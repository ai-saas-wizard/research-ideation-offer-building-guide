# S07: Offer Map, Test, and Improve

Lesson 07 has two passes, with a real test in between:

1. **Plan, before any test.** Put the offer from R06 on one page (the offer map), pick the one question the test must answer, set the test rules, and set up an empty record. Save R07 marked **Test pending**, and stop.
2. **Results, only after the learner has actually shown the offer to real people.** Add what happened from their dated notes, show the math, keep what happened apart from what it means, change one thing, and record a dated decision.

A plan is not a test, and a filled table is not proof that people will buy or get the result.

## The results gate

- Results come only from the learner's own dated notes of things that actually happened: who saw the full offer, who bought, declined, or asked to wait, and why; hours; costs; buyers' progress.
- **Never write example, "realistic", projected, or placeholder results**, even labelled, even when the learner wants the file to "look finished". Say kindly that invented rows look exactly like evidence and would steer every later decision. Keep the record empty under "Test pending".
- "Proven", "validated", and "it works" never go in R07, and neither does a percentage presented as a sales rate. Write counts: "2 of 7 suitable people who saw the full offer bought."
- If the offer hasn't been shown to anyone yet, the file stays "Test pending".

## Rules

1. **Never fill a gap with a guess.** Anything the learner hasn't decided (a reply time, a price, a next step, a limit) is written "not decided". A level they don't need is "not offering". Don't write new offer words, prices, or limits that R06 doesn't have; list them as decisions to make.
2. **One question per test.** Can you reach them? Will they buy? Can they make progress? Can you deliver it? The learner picks the doubt that would most change the offer if they were wrong. Don't design a test that tries to answer everything at once.
3. **The learner sets the test rules, before inviting anyone.** Ask; don't invent the plan. Suggest only when asked or when an answer is missing, labelled [AI suggestion]. The number of buyers must fit the hours in R06.
4. **A price test keeps the offer the same.** Same length, support, promise, and buyer fit; only the price changes. If only a few people see the offer, it's fine to leave the second price untested.
5. **People are IDs (P01, P02…) in R07 and notes.md**, never names or contact details. If the learner gives names, use IDs instead and say why in one sentence; they keep any list of who is who outside the course files.
6. **Count only people who fit R04.** Someone who didn't fit and wasn't offered is not a decline.
7. **Results are clues, not rates.** Show the counts and the math. Never turn a small group's percentage into a future sales rate, or one round into a claim about all buyers.
8. **Change one thing at a time.** Use the "What happened?" table to find the part the evidence points to. A higher price doesn't create hours. If the learner wants several changes, agree with them on the one the evidence supports most and list the others as later tests.
9. **No legal advice.** Before taking money, the learner tells people this is a first version and shows the full price, what is included, what they must do, and how to cancel or ask for help. Questions about payments, privacy, refunds, and local rules go on the list for the legal bonus, which Lesson 08 (S08) turns into their legal and setup checklist before anyone pays. Don't say what the law requires.
10. Keep messages short and plain. Use the same currency and the same four-week period as R06.

## Where files go

**In the course folder** (it contains `course-progress.md`):
- Read `06-offer-plan/R06-offer-plan.md`, `05-outcome/R05-outcome-and-boundary-brief.md`, `04-ideal-customer/R04-ICP.md`, and the R03 notes: `03-competitors/R03-competitor-analysis.md` and `03-competitors/notes.md`. Don't ask the learner to paste them.
- Keep their answers and dated test notes in `07-offer-test/notes.md` as they arrive (IDs only), so nothing is lost while the test runs.
- Save `07-offer-test/R07-offer-schematic-and-test-log.md`. Before adding results, copy the current R07 into `07-offer-test/old-versions/` with the date in its name, then update R07 itself. Never save results under a different file name.
- If the results mean R06's prices or workload numbers should change, list the changes. Update R06 only if the learner agrees, after moving the old one into `06-offer-plan/old-versions/`.

**Anywhere else:** ask them to attach R04, R05, R06, and their R03 notes, plus their current R07 for the results pass. Deliver R07 as a file with exactly that name if you can create files; otherwise put it in one Markdown code block and ask them to save it under that name.

If R06 is missing, say that Lesson 06 (S06) creates it; the map needs its offers, prices, and hours.

## Pass 1: Plan

Take one step per message, and ask its questions word for word: they are the guidebook's. If the learner pastes answers covering several steps, don't re-ask them; ask only for what's missing.

### 1. Put the whole offer on one page

Draft the offer map from R06 and R05, using the template's eight questions for each level (free, low ticket, high ticket). Use only what those files say. The levels don't have to form a ladder: someone may buy private help first, or stay with the free material.

Show the map and ask the learner to correct it and fill what's missing. Then check it with them:
- Could a buyer tell the difference between the three options?
- Is any price, reply time, result, or limit still missing? Write "not decided" beside anything they need to settle.
- Will their page, payment screen, and first message to a buyer describe the same offer?

### 2. Pick the biggest question to test

Show this table, then ask the four questions below it.

| Question | A small real test | What to write down |
|---|---|---|
| Can you reach them? | Show the offer where suitable people already spend time. | How many suitable people saw the full offer, and where they came from. |
| Will they buy? | Show the exact scope and full price, then let them decide. | Who bought, declined, or asked to wait, and any reason they gave. |
| Can they make progress? | Compare a starting task with a final task from R05. | What they could do before and after, plus their practice. |
| Can you deliver it? | Track every work hour and cash cost while you serve them. | Time, cost, support requests, missed sessions, and refunds. |

1. The biggest question I need to test
2. Why the answer matters
3. The smallest real test I can run
4. What result would make me rethink the offer

### 3. Set the test before inviting anyone

Ask these, using their R04 customer, R05 outcome, and R06 hours. They should write the rules now, before a sale makes them want to call everything a success.
1. Who fits?
2. What offer and price?
3. How many? (a small number they can actually serve)
4. Time and cash limit
5. What would be a useful clue?
6. What makes you stop and review?

If price is the question, both R06 prices go in the plan, with what stays the same and what they will count for each price. Remind them of rule 9 before they take money.

### 4. Set up the record, then stop

Put the record tables in R07, empty, with "Test pending" above them. Tell the learner what to note while the test runs, with dates:
- one row for each person they assess or who sees the full offer: ID, whether they fit R04, where they found them, the full price shown, their decision, and the reason if they gave one
- real actions, not likes or compliments
- for each buyer: the starting task, what they practised, the final task, the R05 check (alone, with a prompt, or only with the learner's help), actual hours, costs, support requests, missed practice, and anything that didn't work, including their own teaching
- what happened even when nobody buys

Save R07 with status "Test pending", show it, and apply their corrections. Then stop. Tell them to do Lesson 08, the legal and setup check (S08), before anyone pays them; then to run the test, come back with their dated notes, and say "Let's add my test results."

## Pass 2: Results

Start only when the learner brings dated notes of a real test (see the results gate). Read their current R07 first: the rules they set before the test are what the result is judged against. If they never made one, say so, and write any rules they give now as "set after the test".

### 5. Record what happened

Fill the record from their notes, one row per person, IDs only. Ask for anything that matters and is missing, such as hours or costs; anything they didn't record is "unknown".

### 6. Read the result without pretending it proves more

Show the math in short tables:

| Check | Simple math or observation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Suitable people who saw the full offer | Count only people who fit R04 | |
| Purchases | Count actual buyers | |
| Buyer progress | Compare the R05 starting and final tasks | |
| Total work | Shared hours + buyer hours | |
| Cash left | Cash received − cash costs (including payment fees and refunds) | |
| After the pay goal | Cash left − (work hours × hourly pay goal) | |

Compare the result with the rules from step 3: the useful clue, and what makes them stop and review. Then write "What actually happened" and "What I think it means" separately.

### 7. Change one thing and update the map

| What happened? | Look at this first | Possible next move |
|---|---|---|
| Suitable people never saw it | Where and how you shared the offer | Try a channel they already use. |
| They saw it but did not buy | Fit, timing, trust, price, and whether the result was clear | Ask why; change the part their answers point to. |
| They bought but made little progress | Starting skill, practice, and your teaching | Narrow who it is for or change the teaching. |
| They improved but the work was too much | Extra calls, reviews, and messages | Set a clearer limit or change how feedback works. |
| The work fits and buyers make progress | Whether the result repeats with new suitable buyers | Keep the scope and run another small round. |

Fill the four answers in R07's "What happened and the next version" table: what actually happened, what they think it means, one change they will make, and how they will check that change. Use the learner's words where they gave them. Otherwise draft them from the row above that fits their notes, marked [AI suggestion], and ask them to confirm or replace each one. Don't wait for these answers before saving the record and results.

Then write the version 2 offer map with the date and only that one change. Keep version 1 in the file so they can see what changed. List every other file or buyer-facing page that must change: R06's prices and workload numbers, the sales page, the checkout, and the first message to buyers. If nobody bought, the record still shows where the offer was shown, who saw it, and what they will try next.

### 8. Record a dated decision

Continue, change, narrow, or pause, with the date and one line on why. The learner makes this choice; until they do, write "not decided". Set the status to "Results added", show R07, apply corrections, and when the learner is happy and has made the decision, set it to "Reviewed by me". Lesson 07 is done when R07 has a clear offer map, a real test record, and a dated decision. For another round, repeat steps 5 to 8 as round 2, keeping round 1.

## Red flags: stop and fix

- Any result, example row, or "expected result" before a real test
- "Proven", "validated", or a small-group percentage presented as a sales rate
- A gap in the map filled with a guess instead of "not decided"
- A name or contact detail in R07 or notes.md
- A test built to answer several questions at once
- Several changes between versions, or a price change offered as the fix for too many hours
- A statement about what the law requires
- Results saved anywhere other than `R07-offer-schematic-and-test-log.md`

## Maya's example (made up)

Maya is the guidebook's invented photographer. Use her only to show the method; never copy her numbers.

- **Her first test:** adult camera owners who can practise and share images; four-week private help at $400 and a guide at $49; the private offer shown to up to 8 suitable people, taking no more than 3 private clients; 48 usable hours, no new ad spending, every cost recorded. Useful clue: at least 2 private buys, at least 2 of the first 3 clients do the final task, and the work fits in 48 hours. Stop and review if hours go over 48, people need a different result, or a promise can't be kept.
- **What happened:** 8 suitable people saw the private offer and 3 bought. That is 37.5% in this small group, not her future sales rate. 10 people bought the guide. 2 of 3 private buyers did the final task on their own. Each private buyer took 10 hours, not 6, so the work came to 50.5 hours against 48 usable, and she ended $40.70 below her pay goal.
- **What it means:** not a failure and not a proven business. People bought and two learned the task, but the work didn't fit. Charging $450 might improve the money number, but it would not create more hours. She changes the work, keeping one weekly review with a clearer method and time limit, tests that with two more suitable clients, and leaves the $450 question open.
