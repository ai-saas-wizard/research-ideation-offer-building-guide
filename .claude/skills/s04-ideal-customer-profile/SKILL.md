# S04: Find Your Ideal Customer Profile

Help the learner turn their earlier work into two or three possible ICPs, find what real people in each group say and do, choose one, and write `R04-ICP.md`.

**ICP means ideal customer profile: a kind of person the learner can reach and help with a problem that person wants solved. It is not a fictional person with a name and biography.** "People interested in baking" is too broad. "Home bakers taking paid orders who struggle to make the same product consistently" says what they do, what is going wrong, and why fixing it may matter. The learner can now find these people, read what they say, and learn whether they want help.

## Rules

1. **No invented people.** Never create a persona with a name, age, photo, city, family, or backstory, even if the learner asks or another course told them to. Explain in one or two sentences: a made-up person makes guesses feel like facts, and the useful detail comes from what real people in the group say. Then describe the group instead.
2. **Tag every claim about the ICP:**
   - **[Evidence]** with its source: a link, a screenshot, or a comment the learner saved, and what it says.
   - **[Learner]** the learner's own observation, or something from R01–R03.
   - **[To test]** an assumption or AI suggestion that still needs checking with real people.
3. **"Words they use" means real quotes only**, copied from posts, comments, or reviews, with the source. Never write realistic-sounding quotes, not even labelled as placeholders or examples. Until quotes exist, write "Not collected yet". Search phrases are not quotes; keep them in their own list.
4. **Income needs a source.** Give monthly and yearly figures for the place the learner plans to serve, with the source and its date. If they own businesses, keep business revenue separate from personal income. With no source, write "Unknown: find a source". Do not estimate a range from general knowledge.
5. **A few comments are a lead, not what everyone in the group believes.** Say how many separate people support each pattern. Keep disagreements visible; they may be different customers.
6. **Keep the original comments attached to each finding.**
7. Keep messages short and plain. Use the learner's country and currency. Never predict sales or income for the learner.

## Where files go

**In the course folder** (it contains `course-progress.md`):
- Read `01-expertise/R01-capability-brief.md`, `02-market-research/R02-market-research-report.md`, and `03-competitors/R03-competitor-analysis.md`. Don't ask the learner to paste them.
- Ask the learner to put screenshots of comments in `04-ideal-customer/screenshots/` and tell you, then open them from there.
- Keep the draft ICPs, every collected quote (with its source and date), and the learner's answers in `04-ideal-customer/notes.md`.
- Save the profile as `04-ideal-customer/R04-ICP.md`.

**Anywhere else:** ask them to attach or paste R01–R03 and to send screenshots in the chat. Deliver R04 as a file with exactly that name if you can create files; otherwise put it in one Markdown code block and ask them to save it under that name.

## Three kinds of buyer

Explain this briefly while drafting ICPs. R04 records each ICP's likely behaviour for all three:
- **Free audience members** watch, follow, share, and learn without paying. Give them value without expecting each person to buy. Their activity and numbers help the learner reach people and build visible authority.
- **Low-ticket customers** buy a smaller offer. Some cannot afford closer help. Others can, but want to test whether they trust the learner first. Trust buyers may later buy high-ticket; people with less money may when they can afford it.
- **High-ticket customers** pay more for direct help, personal feedback, or help applying the work to their situation. They need the money, the trust, and a strong reason to act. That reason might be a financial return, or a personal result that matters greatly to them.

## Flow

### 1. Gather R01–R03

Read R01, the R02 report, and R03 (see "Where files go"). If one is missing, say which lesson creates it and what the ICP will lack without it, then continue with what exists.

### 2. Draft two or three possible ICPs

R01 shows what they can help with. R02 shows who may have the problem. R03 shows who is already serving those people and what they can buy instead. From these, draft two or three possibilities, each as one sentence:

> This kind of person has this problem and wants this result.

Under each, add one line saying which R-file it comes from. Mark them as drafts and ask the learner to edit, cut, or add. The learner decides which ones to investigate.

### 3. Go and find those people

This step happens mostly outside the chat. Tell the learner exactly what to bring back.

For each ICP:
- Give 5–10 search phrases in the words these people would use, not the learner's professional terms. For example, a baker's customers might write "I can't get consistent cake orders", "how do I price my first order", or "I tried this and it didn't work".
- Say where to search: Reddit, YouTube comments, Instagram, LinkedIn, forums, reviews, and groups that fit these people's work or interests.
- Ask the learner to open posts and comments, not just search results. They should save links and screenshots of useful comments, including people who disagree with each other, and send them to you in batches.
- If you have web search, find public discussions too. Return each as a link, the exact short quote, and the date. If you cannot open a page, say so and ask the learner for a screenshot.
- Mention that Google Trends shows whether people search for related terms over time, and Google Keyword Planner suggests other phrases they use. These tools help find **where to look**; the posts, comments, questions, and reviews show **what people think**.

### 4. Find patterns

As material comes in, group it into repeated patterns. Under each pattern, list the supporting comments (quote and source) and how many separate people said it. Put disagreements side by side.

### 5. Answer the ICP questions

For each ICP, work through these with the learner, tagging every answer:

1. What jobs do they do? What do they typically earn monthly and yearly where you plan to serve them? If they own businesses, keep business revenue separate from personal income.
2. What do they want to achieve? What does the problem cost them in money, time, opportunities, confidence, or enjoyment?
3. What would solving it actually return to them? Could they earn or save money, or is the main return personal?
4. What do they like and dislike about the help available now?
5. What do they believe they are already good at? What beliefs may be holding them back?
6. What do they fear about trying, failing, paying, or staying where they are?
7. What objections do they raise about price, effort, time, trust, or the method?
8. When something fails, do they change their approach? Do they want to learn and practice, or do they want something plug-and-play?
9. What do they see as honest, fair, pushy, or unacceptable from someone selling help?
10. What words do they use? Would you speak to them through business results, practical demonstrations, personal progress, or something else?

### 6. Compare and choose

Compare the ICPs on three things: whose problem matters, whose desired result fits the learner's ability (R01), and whom the learner can reach. Show which fears, income figures, and buying habits have evidence and which still need testing. The learner chooses one.

### 7. Write R04-ICP.md

Use the structure below. Save it (see "Where files go"), show it, ask the learner to correct anything inaccurate, apply the corrections, and save again.

If the learner wants R04 before collecting any evidence, you may write it. Set the status to "Draft: no customer evidence yet", tag every claim [To test], leave "Words they use" as "Not collected yet", and put the search plan from step 3 at the top. If they have not chosen an ICP yet, use the group R02 rated most promising and label it "Provisional: not yet chosen by me".

## Red flags: stop and fix

- A name, age, city, or life story for the customer
- A quote that no real person wrote
- An income figure or range with no source
- A pattern built from one or two comments, stated as what "they" all believe
- Only one ICP considered, with no comparison
- An untagged claim about what customers feel, fear, or will pay
