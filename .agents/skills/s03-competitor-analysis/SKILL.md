# S03: Competitor Analysis

Help the learner find people who already teach, coach, or consult on something similar to what they want to offer, and see how those people attract customers, what they sell, how they deliver it, and what they charge. The result is `R03-competitor-analysis.md`.

**The learner does the exploring. You help them find more people and turn what they collect into a comparison.** Their own scrolling, clicking, and screenshots are part of the lesson, not a chore to skip.

## Rules

1. **Never make up people, accounts, links, prices, offers, or services.** Every detail in R03 must come from a page you opened in this conversation, or from the learner's links, screenshots, or notes.
2. **Without web access**, do not produce a list of providers with links, prices, or offers from memory. Give search terms instead. You may mention names you already know as leads, but only labelled "from memory, not checked", with no links, prices, or offer details. They go into R03 only after the learner has checked them.
3. **Keep "Seen" and "Unknown" apart for every competitor.** "Seen" says what is visible, where, and on what date. Anything not visible is "Unknown", even if you think you know it.
4. **Prices always carry the currency and the date seen.** If a price is not public, write "Not public".
5. **Use the same headings for every competitor**, so they can be compared.
6. **If you cannot open a link** (an Instagram post, a video, a login page), say so and ask for a screenshot or a description of what they saw.
7. **Include free content and the ways people solve the problem themselves**, not only paid coaches. Aim for about ten options in total.
8. **R03 records what exists; it does not decide strategy.** No conclusions about market gaps, positioning, or what the learner should charge. Later lessons make those decisions.
9. Keep messages short and plain.

## Where files go

**In the course folder** (it contains `course-progress.md`):
- Read `02-market-research/R02-market-research-report.md` and `01-expertise/R01-capability-brief.md`. Don't ask the learner to paste them.
- Ask the learner to put screenshots in `03-competitors/screenshots/` and tell you, then open them from there.
- Record each batch in `03-competitors/notes.md` as it arrives (links, what they saw, dates), so nothing is lost between sessions.
- Save the analysis as `03-competitors/R03-competitor-analysis.md`.

**Anywhere else:** ask them to attach or paste the R02 report and to send screenshots in the chat. Deliver R03 as a file with exactly that name if you can create files; otherwise put it in one Markdown code block and ask them to save it under that name.

## Flow

### 1. Start from R02
Read the R02 report (and R01, see "Where files go"). Confirm which customer-problem group or groups from the shortlist they are studying. If there is no R02, ask for two lines: who the customer is, and what problem the learner helps them with.

### 2. Start with who they already know
Ask: who have they already seen doing similar work, on Instagram, YouTube, or anywhere else? Names or links are both fine.

### 3. Discover more people
- **If you have web search:** search for coaches, consultants, course creators, and other relevant providers currently helping the customers in R02 with those problems. For each, give links to their accounts and offers, and one line on why they are relevant. Label them "found by AI, please check". Do not make up people, prices, or services.
- **If you do not:** give 8–12 search terms built from R02: the problem, the result people want, and words such as "coach", "consultant", "course", or "lessons". Follow rule 2 for any names.

### 4. Give the learner their exploring task
Send this as a short checklist, adapted to their field:

- **Start with people you know.** Open promising accounts and look at who they follow, collaborate with, or mention.
- **Build an Instagram research feed.** Search for people doing similar work. Watch their Reels, rewatch the useful ones, and like or save the ones you want to study. Then, in your normal Reels feed, scroll past unrelated videos and spend your time on videos about your niche, so Instagram shows you more accounts in that area. Save useful Reels and account links as you go; don't rely on remembering them.
- **Do the same on YouTube.** Find channels teaching or selling something similar. Watch a few videos, then check the description, pinned comment, and channel links. Click through to the website or landing page. Where does the creator send viewers: a free resource, a course page, an application, a booking link, or a checkout? Follow the links you can see.
- **For each person, collect:** account and video links; screenshots of their landing page, offer, and price if public; the date and currency; who they serve; what they promise; how they work (one-to-one or group, online or in person, live or recorded); how long the offer lasts; and what support is included.
- **Send links, screenshots, and notes in batches.**

### 5. Record each batch
Add each competitor using the headings in the template. After each batch, tell the learner:
- what is still unknown for each one, and what would fill it
- how many options they have so far out of about ten
- what kinds are missing (for example free content, or do-it-yourself options)

### 6. Write R03
When they have about ten options, write R03 using the template. End with three people whose work they want to study more closely. You may suggest three with a reason each, but the learner makes the choice.

### 7. Review and deliver
Save R03 (see "Where files go"), show it, and ask the learner to check it against their own screenshots and correct anything wrong. Apply the corrections and save again. Lesson 04 (S04) uses R01, R02, and R03 together.

## Red flags: stop and fix

- A provider, link, price, or offer that nobody opened or screenshotted in this conversation
- A price with no currency or no date
- "Approximately" or "roughly" in front of a price you remember
- A competitor profile with fewer headings than the others
- A statement about the market gap, the learner's positioning, or their price
- Profiles that only list paid coaches, with no free or do-it-yourself options
